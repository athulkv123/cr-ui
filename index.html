From sign-up to delivery: how Cryo actually runs

This maps the complete customer lifecycle as the backend implements it today — who creates an account, how a subscription gets approved and billed through MamoPay, how orders are priced against the subscription allowance, and which jobs run automatically every night. Every box below reflects real logic in the codebase, not an idealized version of it.

00

The full lifecycle at a glance

One account moves through four phases: it is created and approved, it gets an active subscription, it places orders against that subscription's allowance, and it renews — either automatically (cash/credit) or by MamoPay charging the customer's card again (payment‑link). Everything below drills into one of these phases.

01

Registration & approval

There is no public sign-up page. Every account — customer, representative, or staff — is created for the person by someone else, and credentials are emailed to them. The only decision point is whether a new customer needs a human to sign off before they're allowed in.

Same pattern, two more account types. A Customer can add up to 5 customer_representative sub-accounts (no approval step — active immediately, inherits the parent's payment mode); an Admin can create sales_team staff the same way. Both simply get credentials emailed straight away — there's no discount gate outside the main customer flow.
02

User types & access

Accounts live in two separate tables with two separate login guards. Everything under a customer's umbrella — the customer and its representatives — is gated by one middleware that checks whether the parent account is active.

03

Subscription & caution deposit

A subscription is a one-month period record (UserSubscription), not a standing flag. Whether that period is "active" is computed on demand from four conditions, and the way a period gets created is completely different depending on how the customer pays.

Known gap worth flagging to engineering. When a customer unsubscribes, the code that would tell MamoPay to stop the recurring charge is written but commented out — cancellation today only stops Cryo's own side from recognizing the subscription as active.
04

Placing an order

Every order is priced against the customer's active subscription pool before anything is charged. Items already covered by the plan's monthly allowance are free; anything beyond it — or not in a pool at all — is billed, plus 5% VAT.

05

MamoPay payments

MamoPay is used four ways: one-off links (orders, deposits), a recurring link (subscriptions), direct charges against a saved card, and a server-to-server webhook that is the only fully trusted source for recurring billing outcomes.

06

Cron jobs & automatic renewal

Two jobs run every night. Payment-link customers don't need either of them for a normal renewal — MamoPay bills them on its own schedule and the webhook does the rest — these jobs exist for cash/credit renewal and for a one-time pricing migration.

Exact run time & timezone. Both jobs are registered with Schedule::call(...)->dailyAt('01:00') / dailyAt('01:30') in routes/console.php with no ->timezone() override, and this environment's .env has no APP_TIMEZONE set either — so Laravel falls back to config('app.timezone')'s default, UTC. That means the jobs actually fire at 01:00 UTC and 01:30 UTC, which is 05:00 and 05:30 Gulf Standard Time (UTC+4). If the production server's .env sets APP_TIMEZONE to something else, the wall-clock time shifts accordingly — worth confirming directly on the production .env before relying on this for support timing.
07

Subscription price update

When plan prices need to go up, an admin runs one command by hand — php artisan subscription:update-price (or with --dry-run first, to preview the exact numbers with no changes made). It does two things: raises every plan's price in the catalog, then rebuilds every payment-link customer's MamoPay link so their next charge reflects the new price.

The formula. New price = (Old price + AED 50) × 1.05 — a flat AED 50 is added to every plan first, and then 5% GST is charged on top of that new subtotal, not just on the AED 50. That's why the real increase is always a bit more than AED 50.
Example plan — "Standard Monthly" AED 200.00
Customer's MamoPay link charges AED 200.00 / month
is_mamo_pay_regenerate flag unchanged
Old price AED 200.00
+ AED 50 flat increase AED 250.00
+ 5% GST on that subtotal AED 12.50
New plan price AED 262.50+ AED 62.50
Customer's MamoPay link charges AED 262.50 / month
Old link deleted
is_mamo_pay_regenerate flag set to true, only if no active subscription

So a AED 200 plan doesn't become AED 250 — it becomes AED 262.50, a real increase of AED 62.50, because GST compounds on top of the AED 50 bump. Every existing plan is shown with its old and new price in a table before the admin is asked to confirm, so nothing changes without a final "yes."

08

Price update → flag → re-subscribe

This is the aftermath of a subscription price change, and it only matters for payment_link customers — cash/credit customers just get billed the new price on their next cron renewal automatically. Two different triggers can mark a payment-link customer as is_mamo_pay_regenerate, but only one of them actually pushes out a new payable link on its own.

Practical implication. Running subscription:update-price updates pricing and every existing link immediately, but any customer it flags (no active subscription at that moment) still needs an admin to hit reinitiate before they see a payable link again. The nightly flag-mamo-pay-regenerate cron is even more hands-off — it only tags lapsed old-price customers during its 14 Jul – 15 Aug 2026 window; it never creates or sends anything by itself. Until admin action happens, that customer's subscription_status stays inactive and they cannot place new orders.
09

Everything on a clock, in one place

Time-based rules are scattered across the sections above — here they're gathered into one plain-English list, alongside the handful of things that are deliberately (or not-so-deliberately) not time-limited today.

WhatHow long / whenIn plain terms
Nightly subscription renewalEvery day, ~5:00 AM Gulf timeCash/credit customers due to renew today get their next month started automatically, no one needs to log in.
Nightly re-subscribe checkEvery day, ~5:30 AM Gulf time, only between 14 Jul – 15 Aug 2026Finds payment-link customers whose plan just lapsed at the old price and tags them so an admin can push out a new link (see §07). It's a one-time cleanup tied to the July 2026 price change, not a permanent job.
How long a subscription period lastsAbout one monthA brand-new cash/credit customer's very first month runs a full calendar month. Every renewal after that — and every payment-link month — runs one day shorter. It's a one-day inconsistency, not something a customer would likely notice, but worth a decision on whether to make it consistent.
Cancelling an order10 hours after it's approvedOnce an order is approved, the customer or an admin has exactly 10 hours to cancel it. After that, the only options are letting it ship or handling it manually.
"Forgot password" linkValid 60 minutes; one new link per 60 secondsStandard password-reset email — the link stops working after an hour, and a customer can't request a fresh one more than once a minute.
Being signed inNever expires on its ownOnce someone logs in, their access doesn't time out — they stay signed in until someone explicitly logs them out. There's no automatic "logged out after being idle" rule.
Invoice due dateSame day it's issuedEvery invoice is marked due the day it's created — there's no "pay within 30 days" style grace window built in.
A subscription that has lapsedNo grace periodThe instant a subscription's end date passes, the customer loses their plan discount on new orders — everything is billed at full price. They are not blocked from ordering, though.
A MamoPay payment linkDoesn't expireA generated payment link stays payable indefinitely until someone deletes or replaces it — MamoPay isn't told to time it out.
Worth a decision before scaling up. Two safeguards you'd normally expect aren't actually active: repeated wrong-password attempts don't lock an account (the check exists in the code but never counts the attempts, so it can't trigger), and there's no cooldown on how fast API requests can be made in general. Neither causes a problem today, but both are the kind of thing to tighten before a large marketing push or public launch.

Notifications
